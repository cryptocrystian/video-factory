# Synthetic Frontier Production Bible v1

Status: **Canonical production doctrine**
Applies to: Synthetic Frontier long-form episodes, Shorts derived from long-form, trailers, and premium channel assets.
Last revised: 2026-09-22

## 1. Purpose

Synthetic Frontier should feel like a premium science documentary produced by a top-tier editorial team, not an AI-generated explainer.

The production system exists to make that standard repeatable. Generative models are production tools, not the aesthetic.

North star:

> **Wonder first. Consequence second. Judgment never.**

Public tagline:

> **Where Intelligence Meets Matter.**

Core production principle:

> **Quality first. Cost second. Premium spend must have a creative reason and must improve accepted production output.**

## 2. Reference stack

Synthetic Frontier does not imitate one producer or show. It combines proven production values from several documentary traditions:

- **NOVA / premium science documentary:** mystery, discovery, scientific rigor, patient visual explanation.
- **HUGE* If True / modern future-tech documentary:** curiosity, optimistic rigor, strong preproduction, visual ambition, warm contemporary design.
- **Vox-style explanatory journalism:** documents, maps, evidence, charts, and motion graphics that transform information into understanding.
- **Premium streaming documentary cinematography:** photographed-feeling environments, real depth, evolving light, physical camera behavior, atmospheric sound, and deliberate pacing.

The result must remain recognizably Synthetic Frontier.

Visual character:

- premium naturalism over overt futurism
- physical/tactile surfaces over glossy synthetic abstraction

Avoid:
- generic AI aesthetics
- glowing blue brains

- faux-HUD interfaces
- gratuitous chrome/sci-fi
- constant motion
- decorative graphics with no explanatory function
- shots that exist only because a model can make them

## 4. Production hierarchy

The show is produced through distinct functions. These may be implemented as skills/passes rather than separate agents.

1. **Showrunner / Story Architect**
   - owns central mystery, audience promise, episode structure, escalation, payoff, and channel consistency.

2. **Research / Standards Editor**
   - owns facts, sourcing, dates, units, claim status, uncertainty, provenance, and factual visual boundaries.

3. **Documentary Director**
   - owns what each scene means emotionally and visually.

4. **Cinematography Director**
   - owns camera, lens behavior, subject motion, parallax, focus, lighting evolution, physical inertia, and shot endpoints.

5. **Production Design & Continuity**
   - owns recurring environments, architecture, materials, object identity, scale, lighting references, and continuity packages.

6. **Editorial Director**
   - owns sequence timing, rhythm, reveals, shot length, visual variation, transitions, and the paper edit/animatic.

7. **Motion Graphics / Evidence-to-Visual Director**
   - owns data visualization, maps, documents, diagrams, typography, compositing, and evidence transformations.

8. **Sound Director**
   - owns narration direction, score map, ambience, SFX, silence, sonic transitions, and final mix intent.

9. **Creative Model Router**
   - chooses model/provider based on the shot's required capabilities, quality threshold, benchmark evidence, reliability, accepted-shot economics, then price.

10. **Documentary QC / Post Supervisor**
   - rejects weak or synthetic-feeling output and owns technical, editorial, continuity, rights, fact, color, and delivery QC.

## 6. Ears / Eyes / Evidence production script

Every episode is authored in three synchronized columns:

| EARS | EYES | EVIDENCE |
|---|---|---|
| narration | exact visual direction | source / claim |
| emotional beat | shot type / motion / transition | date / units / scope |
| timing | real/generated/graphic/document | provenance class |
| emphasis / pause | continuity reference | confidence / caveat |

This is the canonical handoff format into production.

## 7. Shot philosophy

Every shot must do at least one of the following:

- create wonder
- reveal information
- explain a mechanism
- escalate scale or consequence
- provide evidence
- create contrast
- bridge two ideas
- deliver payoff

If it does none, remove it.

A shot must be designed as moving cinematography, not as a still image with motion added.

Every generated hero shot requires:
- visual objective
- narrative purpose
- camera position
- lens character
- camera movement
- subject movement
- foreground/midground/background behavior
- lighting evolution
- focus behavior
- shot endpoint
- continuity reference
- negative constraints

Key question:

> **What changes during this shot?**

If the answer is only “the camera pushes in,” the concept is probably under-directed.

## 8. Visual-source hierarchy

Before generating, ask:

> **Does reality already look better?**

Preferred order by appropriateness, not by cost:
1. real documentary footage
2. licensed/public-domain/owned source material
3. authored graphics/diagrams
4. composited reconstruction
5. generated cinematic footage
6. generated still/keyframe

Generated footage is strongest for:
- inaccessible environments
- things that do not yet exist
- physically impossible camera positions
- reconstructions
- scale transitions
- continuity-preserving speculative visualization

Real footage should dominate where authenticity itself carries value.

## 9. Provenance classes

Every visual must be internally classified:

- **DOCUMENTED** — authentic source or directly documented reality.
- **RECONSTRUCTION** — visualization of a documented event/object/system.
- **CONCEPT** — plausible visualization of a documented proposal or future system.
- **SPECULATIVE** — explicitly hypothetical.

The final edit must never make speculative/generated imagery appear to be documentary evidence.

## 10. Rights classes

Every external asset must have one:

- OWNED
- LICENSED
- PUBLIC_DOMAIN
- FAIR_USE_EDITORIAL
- PERMISSION_REQUIRED
- UNKNOWN

UNKNOWN and PERMISSION_REQUIRED assets cannot enter final master without resolution.

## 11. Continuity doctrine

Recurring subjects require continuity packages.

A continuity package includes:
- canonical wide view
- canonical profile/alternate angle
- dimensions or relative scale
- materials
- geometry
- surface detail
- lighting reference
- color temperature
- prohibited mutations
- reference asset IDs
- approved prompt anchors

Examples:
- Orbital Compute Platform A
- AI Campus A
- Server Rack Architecture A
- Data Hall A
- Power Substation A

A recurring environment is treated as a production asset, not reinvented per shot.

## 12. Editorial pacing

Synthetic Frontier should retain viewers without frantic editing.

Rules:
- shot length follows information and emotional function, not a fixed cadence
- long shots must contain internal evolution
- explanation graphics must remain visible long enough to understand
- hero reveals may breathe
- evidence inserts should be brief and purposeful
- cut on conceptual change, visual completion, or tension—not arbitrary elapsed time

Visual scale should vary:
orbit → macro → industrial → human-scale → aerial → graphic → evidence → environment.

Avoid long stretches in one visual register.

## 13. Motion graphics

Graphics transform information; they do not decorate it.

Preferred behavior:
- isolate a fact from a source
- transform it into a number, map, diagram, or spatial relationship
- use that transformation to bridge into the next shot

Example:
```
FCC filing
→ relevant line isolated
→ “1,000,000” extracted
→ number becomes orbital distribution
→ cinematic constellation shot
```

System components should be reusable:
- source document treatment
- number reveal
- chart
- map
- timeline
- comparison
- power-flow diagram
- lower third
- citation treatment
- chapter card
- scale indicator

## 14. Sound doctrine

Sound is a narrative layer, not finishing garnish.

Each episode requires:
- narration direction
- score map by act
- environmental ambience
- selective SFX
- sonic transitions
- planned silence
- final loudness/peak control

Narration should remain intelligible and natural, not “social-video loud.”

Silence is allowed and should be used to increase impact.

## 15. Color / finishing

Show look:
- warm highlights
- neutral Bone whites
- deep but readable blacks
- restrained saturation
- cool Ice accents only where useful
- consistent material rendering
- no model-by-model grade drift

Generation creates photography. Post creates the film.

Accepted shots may require:
- stabilization
- reframing
- retiming
- cleanup
- masking
- compositing
- color matching
- grain/noise management
- sharpening
- optical-flow work

## 16. Production gates

1. Story approved
2. Fact ledger approved
3. Script approved
4. Visual treatment approved
5. Storyboard/animatic approved
6. Sample production gate passed
7. Full shot plan + cost approved
8. Rough cut approved
9. Picture lock
10. Sound/color/graphics complete
11. Fact/rights/technical QC complete
12. Final master approved
13. Publish authorization

No publishing is implicit.

## 17. Sample production gate

Before full episode production, create a representative 20–60 second sequence that includes the hardest production problems.

Pass standard:

> Could this sequence plausibly sit beside premium network/streaming science documentary work without requiring an excuse for how it was made?

The sample must prove:
- true video-native cinematography
- continuity
- evidence treatment
- graphics
- narration
- sound design
- pacing
- color
- final delivery quality

If the sample fails, do not scale the workflow.

## 18. Production economics doctrine

The factory optimizes **cost per accepted production second**, not raw provider price.

A more expensive model can be cheaper if it:
- produces usable output in fewer attempts
- reduces cleanup
- preserves continuity
- avoids editorial compromise
- creates a signature shot that materially improves perceived production value

A premium model must have an explicit value reason.

## 19. Derivatives

From approved master, derive:
- clean master
- captioned master
- textless master where practical
- transcript
- chapter metadata
- thumbnail assets
- Shorts
- vertical clips
- platform-specific encodes

Derivatives never redefine the master.

## 20. Non-negotiable acceptance principle

> **If the viewer notices the generation technique before the story, the shot fails.**

And:

> **The cheapest rejected generation is the one we never make.**
